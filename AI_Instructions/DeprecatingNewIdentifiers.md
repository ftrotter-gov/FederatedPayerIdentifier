# Deprecating Newly Generated Identifiers

The Federated Payer Identifier originally supported two ways for a payer to obtain an FPI: deriving a deterministic UUIDv5 from an existing payer identifier, or generating a brand-new UUID (v1, v4, v6, v7, or v8) when the payer did not wish to reuse an identifier it already held. That second path has been removed. An FPI must now always be derived, through the two-step chained UUIDv5 process implemented in `tools/FPI_maker_cli.py`, from a legacy identifier the payer already holds in one of the systems enumerated in `reference_data/current_payer_identification_systems.json`. The reason is verifiability: a randomly generated UUID has no auditable connection to the legal payer entity, cannot be independently recomputed by CMS, NPD, or any other consumer, and therefore gives the registration process nothing to check beyond uniqueness. Accordingly, `fpi_source_system` and `fpi_source_value` are no longer optional generation metadata — they are required on every identifier entry with `is_fpi: true`, and an FPI that does not recompute from its declared source is invalid. A payer whose identifier system is not yet on the approved list should submit a pull request adding that system rather than minting a UUID of its own. Throughout the documentation, the term "self-enumeration" (and the related phrase "self-issued") has been replaced by **"payer-selected legacy enumeration"**, which more accurately describes what the payer actually chooses: not the UUID, but which pre-existing legacy identifier anchors it.
